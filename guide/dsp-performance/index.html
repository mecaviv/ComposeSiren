DSP performance: the Rust voices against the C++ voice

Measured 2026-10-12 on one machine (the fast trio the same evening): Mac mini 2018, Intel Core i5-8500B 3.0 GHz, macOS 15.7.9, rustc 1.98.1, Release builds. The C++ is the plugin's own voice sources, built by Source/siren-dsp-oracle with the plugin's Release optimisation (SIREN_ORACLE_OPT=3); the Rust voices are Source/siren-dsp with each named variant (variants.rs).

Reproduce from Source/siren-dsp:

One voice at a time plays a held note with vibrato, rendered in host-sized blocks after a 0.5 s warm-up. Each figure is the median of 7 runs of 2 s of audio: nanoseconds per sample, and in brackets the real-time factor (how many such voices one core could play).

Summary

  • The default build (COMPOSESIREN_RUST_DSP OFF) has no regression: its voice and settings sources preprocess to the same code as before the Rust work (763107c), apart from one constant check in Settings::isAvailable, which isn't on the audio path.
  • rust-exact, which plays the same samples as the C++ bit for bit, now costs about 0.9× the C++ (S3 at 48 kHz: 198 against 220 ns per sample; 1211 against 1454 at 200 partials). It used to cost about 1.6× (352 ns per sample). The gain is from reading each note's frame once per sample instead of once per partial, with the arithmetic unchanged, so the parity tests still pass. rust-time and frames cost the same as rust-exact.
  • The trio variants cost about 5× the C++ (S3: 1041 ns per sample, 20 voices per core at 30 partials; 1720 before the frame lookups were hoisted). They play up to four banks of partials, each with its own libm sin per partial per sample.
  • The fast trio variants (trio-fast, trio-linear-fast, trio-nearest-fast: trio.fast) cost about 2.2× the C++, 2.2 to 2.9× faster than the trio they speed up (S3: 474 against 1041 ns per sample at 30 partials, 2544 against 7360 at 200; 44 voices per core). Their sines come from fastsin.rs, a branch-free polynomial the compiler vectorizes (SSE2/AVX on x86_64, NEON on aarch64; plain Rust, no intrinsics, so the same code is the scalar fallback), and a locked phase is only formed on the samples that are computed. Not bit for bit: tests/fast.rs checks the sine within 1e-9 of libm's (measured 6.6e-10) and every siren within 1e-6 of the peak of the trio it speeds up (measured 1.1e-7, the f32 output's rounding). The lab's phasor kernels (kernels.rs, f32 phasors re-anchored per frame) would re-anchor per frame and can't follow the voice's glide, which changes the pitch every sample; the f32 variants below take their idea instead.
  • The f32 phasor trio variants (trio-f32, trio-linear-f32, trio-nearest-f32: trio.fast and trio.f32) cost about 1.6× the C++ at 30 partials and 1.3× at 200 (S3: 328 against 446 for the fast trio and 1056 for the trio; 1780 against 2257 and 7453 at 200), 64 voices per core at 30 partials. With the phase lock, every bank's partial sounds harmonic h on h·Φ, so one f32 polynomial cos/sin of Φ per computed sample (the kernels' anchor) and complex products, eight harmonics at a time, give every sin(h·Φ); with the fixed data, slot i is harmonic i + 1 and the sines are read as one slice. Plain Rust, vectorized by the compiler; nothing allocated on the audio thread (the table is 512 floats in the voice). Deviation from the trio (tests/fast.rs, 7 sirens at 44.1 and 48 kHz, 4 s each): at most 6.1e-6 of the peak, an SNR of at least 116.3 dB; the test bounds are 3e-5 and 100 dB. The harmonic sines alone are within 2.2e-4 of libm's up to h = 511 (bound 5e-4); the low harmonics, which carry the energy, are much closer.
  • Block size, from 64 to 512 samples, changes nothing; time per sample doesn't depend on the sample rate, so the real-time factor halves at 96 kHz.
  • Loading: the C++ loader takes 7.0 s and 854 MiB for every siren's data. The Rust raw loader takes 0.5 s and 300 MiB, and the compact .sirendata files take 0.18 s and 94 MiB.

MacBook Air M4 (aarch64)

The same tables on a MacBook Air, Apple M4, macOS 15.3, rustc 1.94.1, Release, from a fresh clone at 3b4b308. The crate tests (tests/fast.rs included, with the same measured deviations), the C++ Release builds with COMPOSESIREN_RUST_DSP OFF and ON and their ctests (rust-dsp-parity included) pass there too. Everything is 2.3 to 3.4× faster than on the Mac mini; the fast trio costs 1.6× the C++ there, and the f32 trio about the C++'s cost at 30 partials (95 against 89 ns per sample) and less than it at 200 (621 against 654).
